**You track AI visibility with four methods - manual prompt tests in ChatGPT, Google AI Overviews and AI Mode, AI referral traffic in GA4, the AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools, and AI crawler visits in your server logs. Track each platform separately, because ChatGPT and Google usually cite different pages for the same question.**

Key takeaways:

- **Every AI platform picks its sources in its own way**, so a result in ChatGPT tells you nothing about AI Overviews, and the reverse.
- A manual test on 20-30 buyer prompts is a free starting point, but **one run is a snapshot**, because AI answers change between runs.
- **GA4 shows traffic from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot** by referral source domain.
- **Google counts AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic in the regular Search Console report**, under the "Web" search type, with no separate breakdown.
- Since February 2026, Bing Webmaster Tools has an **AI Performance report with citation counts in Microsoft Copilot**, the only such report published by a search engine itself.

This guide is for SEO specialists and site owners who practice generative engine optimization (GEO) and want to know whether that work pays off. Two checks catch the most problems early - the Bing AI Performance report, which counts Copilot citations, and server logs, where ChatGPT-User and Perplexity-User show which pages AI fetches during conversations. OpenAI also states that sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers. The methods fall into three groups - **direct** (checking citations in AI answers), **analytical** (AI referral traffic in GA4 and Search Console) and **technical** (AI crawler visits in server logs). For each one, we cover what it measures, what it misses and how much time it takes.

## What AI visibility means

AI visibility is **how often your site or brand appears in answers generated by large language models and AI search engines**, measured with three metrics - citations, mentions and referral traffic. A citation is a link to your page as a source of the answer, a mention is your brand name without a link, and traffic means visits from people who clicked a link in the answer. The platforms include ChatGPT, Gemini, Google AI Overviews, AI Mode, Perplexity and Microsoft Copilot.

These three are measured with different methods and do not always move together. **A page can be cited and still get no traffic**, because the AI answer was enough for the user. A brand can be named without a link when the model knows it from training data. So before you pick a method, decide which of the three you want to measure.

| What you measure | What it means | Where to check it |
|---|---|---|
| Citation | A link to your page among the answer's sources | Prompt tests, Bing AI Performance, AI visibility trackers |
| Mention | Your brand name in the answer text | Prompt tests, AI visibility trackers |
| Traffic | Visits from links in AI answers | GA4, server logs |

Platforms also differ in where they get their sources, which tells you where to look when citations are missing:

| Platform | Where it gets sources | Where to check citations |
|---|---|---|
| Google AI Overviews and AI Mode | Google index | Prompt tests, Search Console traffic without a breakdown |
| Gemini | Google Search | Prompt tests |
| ChatGPT | OpenAI search with the OAI-SearchBot crawler and search partners | Prompt tests, GA4, server logs |
| Perplexity | Its own index built by PerplexityBot | Prompt tests, GA4, server logs |
| Microsoft Copilot | Bing index | AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools |

## Why you need to track each AI platform separately

Every AI platform chooses sources in its own way, so **a result in ChatGPT does not carry over to Google, or the reverse**. We checked this with data from CitationOne audits - 366 Polish queries from October 28 to November 25, 2026. For each query we saved the sources of the ChatGPT answer and the Google AI Mode answer to the same question, and for 252 queries the AI Overview sources as well.

The results show how little these lists have in common:

| Platform pair | Average share of common domains |
|---|---|
| AI Overviews and AI Mode | 23.3% |
| AI Mode and ChatGPT | 5.9% |
| AI Overviews and ChatGPT | 5.5% |

The share of common domains is the number of domains cited by both platforms divided by the number of all domains cited by either of them, averaged across queries. **Even two Google features, AI Overviews and AI Mode, share less than a quarter of their sources.** In 62% of queries, ChatGPT did not cite a single domain that Google cited.

The same gap shows up for the audited pages. The page someone audited was cited in the AI Overview for **19% of queries with an AI Overview**, in AI Mode for **16%**, and in ChatGPT for **7%**. It appeared in at least one of the three answers 28% of the time, and in all three only 3% of the time.

Three caveats apply to these numbers. These are queries CitationOne users chose to audit, not a random sample of the web, and they come from the Polish market. Each query has one measurement, and citations can change between runs. The numbers show the scale of the differences between platforms, not a stable share for any domain.

> [!REMEMBER]
> ChatGPT and Google cite almost entirely different pages for the same query. AI visibility differs from platform to platform, so each one needs its own measurement.

## How to check AI visibility manually

A manual AI visibility check, including visibility in ChatGPT, Perplexity and Google, means **asking the same prompts on every platform and recording whether your page appears among the sources**. It is free and a good place to start, but with dozens of prompts each round takes a few hours.

1. **Build a set of 20-30 prompts.** Mix branded prompts ("what does [brand] do") with unbranded buyer prompts ("best [product] for [use case]", "alternatives to [competitor]"). Pull real questions from sales calls, support tickets, Search Console queries with many impressions and "People also ask".
2. **Run them on every platform.** ChatGPT with search, Google with AI Overviews and AI Mode, Perplexity and Copilot. Use a new window without chat history to limit the influence of earlier prompts.
3. **Log the results in a spreadsheet.** For each prompt and platform pair, note the citation, the mention and the competitors cited instead of you.
4. **Repeat every 2-4 weeks.** One run is a snapshot, and a trend only shows after several.

A simple tracking sheet looks like this:

| Prompt | Platform | Date | Cited | Mentioned | Cited instead of you |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| how to plan internal linking | ChatGPT | 2026-11-25 | No | No | domain-a.com, domain-b.com |
| how to plan internal linking | AI Overviews | 2026-11-25 | Yes | - | - |

A manual test has two limits. **AI answers vary between runs**, because the system writes each answer from scratch and retrieves sources live, often with different helper queries, so one missing citation does not settle anything. Results also depend on location and language, and AI Overviews appear only for some queries.

## How to track AI traffic in GA4 and Search Console

AI referral traffic tracking in Google Analytics 4 relies on **referral source domains** such as chatgpt.com, perplexity.ai, copilot.microsoft.com, gemini.google.com and claude.ai. In the Traffic acquisition report, choose the "Session source" dimension and filter for these domains. For ongoing tracking, create a **custom "AI" channel group** with a source condition that matches this regular expression:

`chatgpt\.com|perplexity\.ai|copilot\.microsoft\.com|gemini\.google\.com|claude\.ai`

Links from ChatGPT often carry a `utm_source=chatgpt.com` parameter, which GA4 reads as the source. OpenAI does not document it, so treat it as a convenience, not a guarantee. Some clicks from mobile apps reach GA4 without a referrer and land in direct traffic, so **GA4 shows a lower bound of your AI traffic**.

Google is harder, because AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic has no separate category in either tool:

- **Search Console.** Google says pages shown in AI Overviews and AI Mode are reported in the Performance report under the "Web" search type, together with the rest of your search traffic, and no filter separates clicks from an AI Overview.
- **GA4.** Clicks from AI Overviews and AI Mode land in regular Google organic traffic, with the same source as clicks on regular results.

> [!REMEMBER]
> GA4 shows traffic from ChatGPT, Perplexity and Copilot by source domain, but neither GA4 nor Search Console separates AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic from regular Google results.

## The AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools

Since February 10, 2026, Bing Webmaster Tools has an **AI Performance report that shows citations of your site in AI answers** - in Microsoft Copilot, AI summaries in Bing and select partner integrations. It is the only free citation report published by a search engine itself.

The report shows three main metrics:

- **Total citations** in AI answers over the selected period.
- **Average cited pages** from your site per day.
- **Grounding queries**, the phrases the AI used when it retrieved content that ended up in its answers.

Bing keeps expanding the report and has since added topics and citation share. The grounding queries list is the most useful part, because it shows **which questions actually lead AI to your site**, and these are often different from your Search Console queries. If your site is not in Bing Webmaster Tools yet, you can import it from Google Search Console in a few minutes.

## How to find AI crawlers in your server logs

In your server logs, filter for the User-Agent tokens of **ChatGPT-User and Perplexity-User, which fetch pages during a conversation with a user**, and of the indexing crawlers OAI-SearchBot and PerplexityBot, then check that they get a 200 status and not a 403 or 429 block. Logs show AI crawler visits, not citations. OpenAI and Perplexity separate both kinds of bots in their documentation:

| Crawler | Owner | What a visit means |
|---|---|---|
| OAI-SearchBot | OpenAI | Indexing pages for ChatGPT search |
| ChatGPT-User | OpenAI | ChatGPT opened the page because a user asked a question |
| PerplexityBot | Perplexity | Indexing pages for Perplexity results |
| Perplexity-User | Perplexity | Perplexity fetched the page in response to a user action |

Visits from **ChatGPT-User and Perplexity-User mean someone just asked a question that made the system fetch your page**. They do not tell you whether the page was cited, but they show which URLs are within reach of AI answers and how often. OpenAI notes that ChatGPT-User is not used to crawl the web automatically and that robots.txt rules may not apply to it, because a user initiates the visit.

You can spot these crawlers by part of the User-Agent header, for example `ChatGPT-User/1.0` or `OAI-SearchBot/1.4`. Filter for them in your logs and check the status codes - **a 200 means the crawler got the page, while a 403 or 429 means a firewall** or rate limit blocked it, even when robots.txt allows it.

No OAI-SearchBot visits for several weeks is a signal to check your robots.txt and firewall. OpenAI states that **sites that opt out of OAI-SearchBot will not be shown in ChatGPT search answers**. We cover the setup in [how to rank in ChatGPT](/blog/how-to-rank-in-chatgpt).

## AI visibility tracking tools

AI visibility tracking tools, also called LLM tracking tools, **run a set of prompts across several platforms automatically and log citations and mentions** day after day. They replace the manual spreadsheet and show the trend. Typical metrics are share of voice, meaning brand visibility in AI answers measured as the share of answers that mention your brand, the number of citations, position in the answer and sentiment. Sentiment shows whether the model describes your brand favorably, neutrally or with factual errors, and errors are worth logging separately, because they usually point to an outdated or unclear source page.

There are two kinds of tools on the market - AI visibility modules added to large SEO suites, and standalone tools focused only on AI answers. Suites combine the data with keywords and backlinks, while standalone tools usually cover more platforms and repeat prompts more often. Useful features in either kind are scheduled prompt runs, tests from several countries, sentiment tagging, alerts when a competitor gets cited instead of you, and data export. Before you choose one, check four things:

- **Where the answers come from.** The interface users see or a model API, which can return different sources than the browser version.
- **How many times the tool runs the same prompt.** A single daily run makes results noisy.
- **Which location and language it uses.** Answers differ by market and language.
- **Who picks the prompts.** Share of voice on prompts chosen around your brand inflates the score.

"If AI answers change so much, is tracking even worth it?" It is, as long as you look at the trend across many prompts and many days. One prompt on one day is noise, but **a drop in share of voice from 30% to 10% across 50 prompts in a month is a signal** you cannot ignore.

## What to do when AI does not cite your page

When AI does not cite your page, **first check whether the page is within reach of the system at all, and only then look at content quality**. The order matters, because the best text will not be cited if the crawler cannot fetch it.

1. **Access and indexing.** The page must be indexed by Google to appear in AI Overviews and AI Mode, and robots.txt and your firewall must not block OAI-SearchBot. Site structure helps here, as described in our guide to [building an internal linking strategy](/blog/internal-linking-strategy).
2. **An answer to the question.** The section you want cited should open with the answer and make sense when cut out of the article. You will find examples in [how to write content for AI search](/blog/how-to-write-content-for-ai-search).
3. **Related questions.** AI systems break a question into subtopics and look for sources for each of them. A page that answers more of them has more passages to cite, which is what the [fan-out coverage](/dimensions/query-fan-out) dimension measures.
4. **Comparison with cited pages.** See how the pages cited instead of yours differ from your page, in facts, structure and freshness.

The [CitationOne audit](/how-it-works) handles the last step. For a chosen query it shows the ChatGPT, AI Mode and AI Overview answers with their cited sources, flags whether your page is among them, and compares your text with the top 10 across 10 content quality dimensions.

In my view, the most common mistake in tracking AI visibility is **checking one platform and treating the result as a verdict on your whole GEO effort**. A page cited in an AI Overview can be invisible to ChatGPT, and our data suggests that this is the rule rather than the exception. Tracking only makes sense when it covers the platforms your customers actually use.

## Frequently asked questions

Tracking AI visibility combines free manual checks on a fixed set of prompts with GA4 and server log monitoring. These are the most common questions about it.

### Can you check AI visibility for free?

Yes. Manual prompt tests in ChatGPT, Google and Perplexity, a source filter in GA4, the AI Performance report in Bing Webmaster Tools and your server logs cost nothing but time. Paid tracking tools save that time and show trends across many days, but they do not measure anything you cannot check by hand.

### How often should you track AI visibility?

Repeat a manual check every 2-4 weeks on the same set of prompts so results stay comparable. Check more often after big changes, such as publishing a new page, updating content or changing robots.txt. Tracking tools run daily, which helps you tell a real trend from a random change.

### Why does ChatGPT show different sources every time?

ChatGPT generates each answer from scratch and can send different helper queries to search, so the source list changes. Location, language and chat history also play a part. That is why one test is not enough - ask the same prompt 3-5 times in new windows and count how many answers include your page.

### Does Search Console show AI Overviews traffic?

Search Console includes AI Overviews and AI Mode traffic, but not separately. Google counts it in the Performance report under the "Web" search type, together with clicks on regular results. No filter shows only AI Overview clicks, so you have to check AI Overview citations with prompt tests.

### What is the difference between a citation and a mention in AI?

A citation is a link to your page among the sources of an AI answer, and it can bring traffic. A mention is your brand name in the answer text without a link, often from what the model learned in training. A mention builds awareness but brings no clicks, so track the two separately.
